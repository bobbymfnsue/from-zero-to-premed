# from zero to pre-med

plain-language science, critical thinking, and practical tools for nontraditional pre-med students starting from the beginning.

## what this is

from zero to pre-med is an open-source learning project built from the perspective of someone who is also starting near ground zero.

the goal is not to pretend difficult science is simple. the goal is to make the path into difficult science easier to understand.

this project will build from plain-language explanations into real scientific terminology, problem solving, and eventually the level of understanding needed for pre-med coursework and medical school preparation.

## who this is for

this project is for students who:

- are interested in medicine but do not come from a strong science background
- are returning to school or taking a nontraditional path
- feel like textbooks sometimes assume they already know things they were never taught
- want to understand concepts instead of only memorizing them
- want to use tools like ai without becoming dependent on them

## how concepts will be taught

when possible, topics will follow the same general path:

1. **start from zero** — assume very little prior knowledge
2. **plain language** — explain the idea in normal words first
3. **make it stick** — use analogies, examples, patterns, or memory tools
4. **learn the scientific language** — connect the simple explanation to the terminology used in class and textbooks
5. **understand why it matters** — connect the concept to later science and medicine
6. **check your understanding** — move from recall to application
7. **verify** — use reliable sources to check what was learned
8. **explain it independently** — make sure the idea still makes sense without relying on ai or notes

## ai and critical thinking

this project treats ai as a tool for learning, not as a replacement for thinking.

a basic workflow is:

**ask → clarify → challenge → verify → apply → explain independently**

that means using ai to help understand difficult material while also learning how to question answers, check sources, notice oversimplifications, and recognize when information may be wrong.
